Receptive & Expressive Language in Children

Language is how we understand others and share our own thoughts, needs, and ideas. Here's some general information about language difficulties in children and how speech therapy can help.

Receptive vs. expressive language

Receptive language

Understanding: following directions, understanding questions, learning new words, and making sense of stories and conversations.

Expressive language

Using language: vocabulary, putting words together into sentences, asking questions, and telling stories or explaining ideas.

Some children have difficulty mainly with one, and some with both. Language is separate from speech clarity: a child can pronounce words clearly and still have trouble with language, or the other way around.

What is a language disorder?

A language disorder means a child has ongoing difficulty understanding and/or using language compared with other children their age. When this happens without a known cause, such as autism, hearing loss, or an intellectual disability, professionals may use the term developmental language disorder (DLD). Language difficulties can also affect reading, writing, and learning at school, which is why support can be helpful at any age, from toddlers to teens.

Things parents sometimes notice

These are general examples only, and many children show some of them from time to time:

  • Trouble following directions, especially ones with more than one step
  • Often answering "what?" or seeming not to understand questions
  • A smaller vocabulary than other children their age, or frequent use of words like "thing" and "stuff"
  • Short or grammatically unusual sentences
  • Difficulty telling a story or explaining what happened in order
  • Struggles with reading comprehension or writing at school

How speech therapy can help

Language therapy is built around each child's needs, interests, and age. Depending on the child, it may focus on:

  • Understanding and following directions and questions
  • Building vocabulary and word-finding skills
  • Using longer, more complete sentences
  • Storytelling, describing, and explaining (narrative skills)
  • Language skills that support reading, writing, and classroom learning
  • Coaching parents and teachers on strategies to use throughout the day

For school-age children, we're happy to coordinate with teachers, with your permission, so strategies carry over into the classroom. Learn about sessions at school.
