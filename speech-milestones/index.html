Speech & Language Milestones by Age

"How many words should my child be saying?" is one of the most common questions parents ask. This general guide shares communication milestones that most children reach by certain ages, adapted from the CDC's Learn the Signs. Act Early. program.

How to use this guide

Milestones describe what most children (about 75%) are doing by a certain age. Every child develops at their own pace, and missing one item doesn't necessarily mean there's a problem. If your child isn't meeting milestones, has lost skills they once had, or you simply have a concern, talk with your child's doctor or a speech-language pathologist. Trust your instincts. You know your child best.

12 months

1 year
  • Waves "bye-bye"
  • Calls a parent "mama," "dada," or another special name
  • Understands "no" (pauses briefly or stops when you say it)

15 months

  • Tries to say one or two words besides "mama" or "dada"
  • Looks at a familiar object when you name it
  • Follows directions given with both a gesture and words
  • Points to ask for something or to get help

18 months

1½ years
  • Tries to say three or more words besides "mama" or "dada"
  • Follows one-step directions without a gesture, like "give me the toy"

2 years

24 months
  • Points to things in a book when you ask, like "Where is the bear?"
  • Says at least two words together, like "more milk"
  • Points to at least two body parts when asked
  • Uses more gestures than just waving and pointing, like blowing a kiss or nodding yes

30 months

2½ years
  • Says about 50 words
  • Says two or more words together, with one action word, like "doggie run"
  • Names things in a book when you point and ask "What is this?"
  • Says words like "I," "me," or "we"

3 years

36 months
  • Talks with you in conversation using at least two back-and-forth exchanges
  • Asks "who," "what," "where," or "why" questions
  • Says what action is happening in a picture or book when asked
  • Says their first name when asked
  • Talks well enough for others to understand, most of the time

4 years

48 months
  • Says sentences with four or more words
  • Says some words from a song, story, or nursery rhyme
  • Talks about at least one thing that happened during their day
  • Answers simple questions like "What is a coat for?"

5 years

60 months
  • Tells a story they heard or made up with at least two events
  • Answers simple questions about a book or story after you read it
  • Keeps a conversation going with more than three back-and-forth exchanges
  • Uses or recognizes simple rhymes, like bat/cat or ball/tall

Simple ways to encourage language at home

These everyday ideas are general suggestions many families enjoy. They are not a substitute for professional guidance for your child:

  • Follow their lead in play. Join in with what your child is interested in.
  • Talk about what you're doing. Narrate everyday routines like cooking, bath time, and getting dressed.
  • Read together. Point to pictures, name things, and let your child turn the pages.
  • Introduce new words in everyday routines. Snack time, walks, and car rides are full of chances.
  • Pause and wait. Give your child time to respond or take a turn.
  • Sing songs and nursery rhymes in any language your family speaks.
