In-Home Speech Therapy for Kids in Beverly Hills & West LA

Speech therapy that happens in your living room, your child's classroom, or the neighborhood park, wherever your child talks, plays, and learns every day.

Why in-home speech therapy?

Most of a child's communication happens at home: asking for a snack, playing with a sibling, telling a parent about their day. In-home speech therapy brings support directly into those moments. Many families find it helps in a few ways:

  • Comfort. Children are often more relaxed and willing to try new things in a familiar place.
  • Real-life practice. We use your child's own toys, books, and routines, so new skills are practiced where they'll actually be used.
  • Parent involvement. You can watch, join in, and ask questions, then use the same strategies all week.
  • Convenience. No commute, no waiting room, and no packing up siblings for an appointment.

What to expect during a home visit

Your therapist arrives with a bag of carefully chosen activities and a plan based on your child's goals. A typical session might include:

  • A warm-up through play to connect with your child
  • Targeted activities for speech, language, or social communication goals
  • Practice built into everyday routines, like snack time or getting dressed
  • A few minutes at the end to talk with you about how the session went and what to try this week

You don't need a special room or materials. A quiet corner, a table, or space on the floor works well.

Speech therapy at school or daycare

For some children, the classroom is where communication goals matter most: following directions, asking for help, joining in play with classmates. With permission from your child's school or daycare, sessions can take place on site. With your consent, we can also coordinate with teachers so strategies carry over through the school day.

Please note: private sessions at school depend on each school's policies. We're happy to help you check what's possible.

Speech therapy in the community

Parks, playgrounds, and neighborhood outings are full of natural chances to communicate: greeting a friend, taking turns on the slide, asking for a snack, or commenting on a passing dog. Community sessions help children practice skills in the busy, real-world places they visit every week.
