Therapy in the languages your family speaks

Los Angeles is home to one of the largest Persian communities outside Iran, and many children here grow up hearing Farsi with grandparents, English at school, and a mix of both at the dinner table. Elora Barsiasa, M.S., CCC-SLP, speaks Farsi and offers speech therapy in both English and Farsi.

Working with a therapist who speaks your family's language can make it easier to:

  • Understand how your child communicates in both languages
  • Include grandparents and Farsi-speaking caregivers in sessions and home practice
  • Share questions and concerns in the language you're most comfortable using
  • Choose activities, books, and songs that reflect your family's culture

Common questions from bilingual families

Will speaking two languages confuse my child or cause a speech delay?

This is one of the most common worries we hear. According to ASHA, learning two languages does not cause speech or language disorders, and children around the world grow up speaking more than one language. Bilingual children may follow a slightly different path, for example mixing words from both languages, which is a typical part of bilingual development.

Should we stop speaking Farsi at home?

Families often hear this advice, but it's a decision to make with a qualified professional who knows your child. Many SLPs encourage parents to keep using the language they speak most comfortably and fluently. During an evaluation, we'll talk about your family's languages and goals together.

How do you tell the difference between learning English and a true delay?

A careful evaluation looks at your child's skills across all the languages they use, not just English. That's one reason a bilingual evaluation can be so helpful. Learn about evaluations.
