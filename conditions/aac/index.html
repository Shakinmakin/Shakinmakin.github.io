AAC: Augmentative & Alternative Communication

Every child deserves a way to communicate. AAC gives children who are not yet speaking, or whose speech is hard to understand, another way to share their needs, ideas, and personality.

What is AAC?

Augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) is any form of communication other than talking. Augmentative means adding to speech; alternative means used in place of speech. We all use some AAC, like waving goodbye or pointing at a menu.

AAC for children can include:

  • No-tech: gestures, sign language, and facial expressions
  • Low-tech: picture cards, communication boards, and books
  • High-tech: speech-generating devices and AAC apps on a tablet

Who uses AAC?

AAC can support children with many different communication needs, including some autistic children, children with childhood apraxia of speech, children with genetic or neurological conditions, and children whose speech is not yet meeting their communication needs. Some children use AAC for a short time, others use it long term, and many use it alongside speech.

A common worry: will AAC stop my child from talking?

This is one of the most common questions parents ask. Research has not found that AAC keeps children from learning to talk. For many children, AAC can support language growth and reduce frustration by giving them a reliable way to be understood. Your SLP can talk through your specific questions about your child.

How speech therapy supports AAC

Depending on the child and family, AAC support may include:

  • Exploring which type of AAC might fit your child's needs and abilities
  • Teaching children to use their AAC system to request, comment, ask questions, and connect
  • Modeling: showing your child how to use the system during real activities
  • Coaching parents, siblings, and teachers so AAC is used throughout the day
  • Building language skills over time, from single words to sentences

AAC works best when it's used everywhere, which is why in-home and school sessions can be so valuable for AAC users.
